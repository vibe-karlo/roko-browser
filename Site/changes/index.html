0.1.4

Version 0.1.4 learns to undo.

7 November 2026

Everything new since 0.1.3.

The bar and your tabs

  • The bar names the site you're on, once its field has stepped aside.
  • Turned sideways, the bar is under the page there too, the page is a rounded card, and the bar folds away as you scroll.
  • The tab switcher has less on it. Private, + and a menu along the bottom, with Today, To-do, Settings and Close All Tabs in the menu, and "3 of 12" to say where you are.
  • Swiping the bar follows your finger and stops with a tick at the first and last tab.
  • With no tab open, the bar is a search field under a rounded card, with Today, News, Saved and To-do within reach of your thumb.
  • A mic in the bar's field, for anyone who hasn't found that holding + listens.

Take it back

  • Undo for what you remove. A page from Saved, a to-do, a page or a whole site from history, a site from News: each offers it for a few seconds. A full swipe no longer removes a page from Saved.
  • Pulling down to close News, Saved, To-do or Today happens as you let go. Move back first and it stays.

News

  • Don't Show. Hold a story to stop seeing its site, whatever brought it there. Undo takes it back, and Sources lists the sites you've hidden.
  • Pictures stay whole. A lead picture could turn into a strange zoomed crop moments after it appeared.
  • It says what happened. A look that found nothing tells you it couldn't reach your sites, rather than that there's no news.
  • Done in Sources adds a site you typed but didn't add, or stays open to say it couldn't find it.
  • Newsletters can't be lost by closing roko in the middle of a refresh.
  • News and Saved are easier to find, in the switcher's menu, and typed in full in the bar.

Reader

  • Whole articles. Reader could leave out sections or start halfway through, on Ars Technica and the BBC among others. It now keeps the whole story, and so does Copy as Markdown.
  • BBC stories open in Reader outside the UK, where they could fall back to the web page, and they lose the labels written for screen readers.
  • When Reader or a summary fails, you get Try again in place of grey text, and Reader also offers the page itself.

Notify Me

  • Real news only. "Just now" becoming "1m ago" no longer counts, and neither do cookie notices or a page caught halfway through moving itself on.
  • Pages behind bot protection are read again, with their scripts running, once a check has been missed.
  • A page that keeps failing is tried less and less: after 3 hours, then 6, 12 and once a day until it reads.

Lighter

  • News needs far less memory, about 40 to 60 MB with it open, down from about 135 MB.
  • Comparing stories, matching topics and checking watched pages no longer hold up the screen.
  • Each letter you type in the bar searches only what still matches, so history answers faster.
  • The microphone stops by itself after 8 seconds with no words, after a minute in all, and whenever you leave.

iPhone Duo

  • Slightly open, the new bar runs across the screen with its controls on one half, and the switcher's row keeps off the bend.

Also

  • A new welcome, in place of seven pages: a short result from our test, then the real browser with a few words over it.
  • roko wears the website's colours. A warm near-black and off-white, one red-orange, and a grainy wash of light behind Today, To-do, Shopping and Saved, in light and dark.
  • News, Saved, To-do, Today and the bar's rows follow your iPhone's Text Size. With Reduce Motion on, screens fade in rather than slide.
  • An unsafe page has a way out: Go back, or Close this tab.
  • Fixed: a few rare replies from online services could stop the app.
0.1.3

Version 0.1.3 brings News and a new bar.

7 November 2026

Everything new since 0.1.1.

News

  • One page that ends. What you follow, each event once. Stories several sites covered open as a comparison of what they agree on and where they differ.
  • Follow almost anything. From a page's tools, or type "follow" in the bar: the section you're on or the whole site, with or without a feed.
  • Newsletters, by their feed, or through a private address to subscribe with or forward to.
  • Topics and words to skip. Topics look across the web, and words you keep out stay out, whichever site a story is from.
  • Reader, Summary and the page itself in one switch. Links work in Reader too, and they open in a tab.

The bar

  • It lies under the page, on a dim shade of the site's colour, with the page as a rounded card above it.
  • What you open most comes first, typing or not.
  • The most visited sites in your country are suggested as you type an address.

Saved

  • Gathered by why you kept a page or by when. Opened pages stay in their group, marked as read.
  • Search is the bar's main action, and Summarize all sits beside it.
  • A way back. A page opened from Saved has an arrow in the bar that returns to the group or search you left.

Cleaner pages

  • Anti-adblock walls don't come up, on about 8,200 sites, with uBlock Origin's site rules.
  • Other "turn off your ad blocker" notices are taken down, in eight languages, and the page scrolls again.
  • More cookie walls answered, in more languages, and never with a no that leads to a payment page.
  • Pages left blank by a wall fill in sooner, with their ads still blocked.
  • Empty ad boxes and bottom ad strips fold away in more places.

Faster

  • Looping muted videos wait until they're near the screen, so a heavy front page uses far less data before you scroll.
  • Tab pictures take a third of the memory, and the bar's history search no longer slows down as history grows.

Also

  • Croatian, Serbian and Bosnian summaries and comparisons use the sites' own sentences rather than a translation.
  • Links from other apps open over whatever is on screen.
  • Fixed: an answer with a big number at the top could crash roko.
0.1.1

Version 0.1.1 cooks and goes dark.

5 November 2026

Everything new since the first public version.

Recipes

  • Cook Mode. On a page that publishes a recipe, one step at a time in big text, with the ingredients to tick off and the screen kept awake. Open it from the page menu. Amounts and steps are the site's own, not written by AI.
  • Timers that ring. Tap a time in a step and it counts down on screen, on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, then rings as an alarm, through silent mode and with roko closed.

Pages

  • Force dark pages. Every site goes dark with no white flash first, and a site that's already dark stays as it is. Turn it on in Settings > Appearance, and leave out any site you like.
  • Save as PDF. The whole page, from the page menu, kept in Downloads.
  • Sign-in and payment windows work. A window a page opens can hand back your sign-in, close itself and return you to that page.
  • No blank tab when a link redirects to another app.
  • No gap under what a page pins to the bottom when the bar folds away.

Tabs and search

  • Close all your tabs at once. Hold the tabs button in the bar. Undo brings them back with their history, and private tabs stay until they're all that's left.
  • Search with Qwant, or an engine of your own. A name and an address, in Settings > Search engine.
  • iPhone search finds your pinned sites and opens your to-do and shopping lists. What's on the lists stays out of it.
  • A search field in the bar when roko opens with your tabs. It turns back into the + once you scroll.
  • Turned sideways, tab cards sit side by side.

Faster and cleaner

  • Pages load sooner. Cookie rules now load after the page rather than before it, so a known pop-up can show for a moment before it's answered.
  • Links that open roko start their page about 80 ms sooner.
  • More ads and trackers blocked, including hosts the usual lists miss. In our test, roko stopped 102 of 122.
  • The first launch after an update is as quick as any other, unless the block lists changed.

iPhone Duo

  • The page fills the screen when you hold it open sideways, with the bar floating over it and folding away as you scroll.
  • Reader is a book. A page each side of the fold, a real page curl, and text size down the outer edge.
  • Side by side at the fold. A summary beside its live page, a Browse for Me source beside its answer, and Read Later as a list beside the article.
  • Tabs are a grid of wide cards, each with its name and a close button under it.
  • Propped open, Cook Mode's step stands above the fold and the rest lies below it.
  • Lying flat, the bar's field and search run across the screen, and the page menu opens in the middle.
  • Messages no longer straddle the fold.

Also

  • Open source. The code is on GitHub under GPL-3.0, and About roko links each credit.
  • Sharing an answer as a public page is gone. Pages you already shared can still be taken down in Settings.
0.1.0

Version 0.1.0 is on public TestFlight.

4 November 2026

The first version anyone can install, so this is everything it came with.

The bar

  • One bar across the app, shaped for the screen it's on.
  • It knows what you mean. The first row is the site you named, Browse for Me for a question, or a new to-do for words that read like one.
  • Answers as you type. Weather, sums, units, rates, time zones, days until, your next event. Pin the ones you want to keep.
  • Hold + and say it. A site's name opens the site, a question goes to Browse for Me, anything else lands in the bar.
  • Pages open the moment you press Go. The top row starts loading while you type.

Summaries and answers

  • Pull down to summarize. A long pull from the top turns the page into its summary.
  • Browse for Me. Ask something and roko reads the web and writes the answer, with its sources and room for follow-ups.
  • Shaped by what they're about. A recipe gets a checklist and steps, a comparison a side-by-side table, a price question the price first.
  • Apple's model on your iPhone, with no account. Add your own Claude API key if you want one.
  • Notify Me. Say what to look for on a page, and roko checks it and tells you.

Clean pages

  • Ads and trackers blocked, with a switch for each site. Empty ad boxes fold away.
  • Cookie pop-ups answered. Rejected wherever a site allows it, accepted only where it needs them.
  • Hide Distracting Items. Tap something twice and it stays gone on that site.
  • Clean links. Copy Link and Share leave out what only says where a link came from.

Tabs and pages

  • Split View. Two tabs on screen, with a divider that stays where you let go.
  • Mini player. Sound and video in a tab you've left get a slim player under the page, with Picture in Picture.
  • Peek at links. Touch and hold one to see its page live.
  • Closed tabs are a tap from coming back, with their history.
  • Swipe anywhere on a page to go back or forward, and pinch in to see your tabs.
  • Forms stay safe. What you typed comes back if a page crashes. Passwords and card numbers are never kept.
  • Read Later. Pages kept with their text, gathered by subject and summarized together.
  • Downloads. Progress, pause and resume, also in the Files app.
  • Translate any page, into the languages you choose.
  • Private tabs say so in the bar, offer nothing of yours, and can lock behind Face ID.

Your day

  • Today. Weather, calendar, to-dos and top stories, first thing each day.
  • To-do. One list that sorts itself. Type it the way you'd say it, and it stays the same as Apple Reminders if you like.
  • Shopping lists that sort into aisles, in English or Croatian, and learn what you buy.
  • Home Screen widgets. To-dos to tick off, the weather, any answer you pinned.
  • iCloud sync for pins, history and settings, through your own account.
  • Sync with your Mac, through roko's Raycast extension. To-dos and shopping lists stay the same, and links you send open as tabs.
  • roko from outside it. Control Center, the Lock Screen, the Action button, Shortcuts, Siri and the share sheet.

iPhone Duo

  • Controls keep off the fold, and Split View parts at it.
  • Propped open like a small laptop, the page stands above and the lower half is its trackpad.
